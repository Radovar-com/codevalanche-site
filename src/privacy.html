Privacy Policy

How the Codevalanche desktop app, the codevalanche.com website and the Codevalanche account handle your data.

1. Who we are

Codevalanche is made by Radovar ("we"). We decide how the data described here is used (data controller). Privacy questions: info@radovar.com. Security issues: security@radovar.com.

This policy covers the Codevalanche desktop app for Windows, the codevalanche.com website and the Codevalanche account.

2. The short version

Codevalanche is a workspace for the AI tools you choose: Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI, or a model running on your own computer. Your conversations, files and code do not pass through our servers. They go straight from your computer to the AI provider you picked, or never leave your computer if you use a local model.

We only receive what is needed for your account, the feedback you choose to send, and, only if you turn it on, a few anonymous usage signals. We do not sell data, we do not use it for advertising, and we do not profile you.

3. What we receive and why

  • Account details: email address, display name, optional short bio (up to 500 characters) and sign-in method (email or Google), when you create and use an account. Your password is held only as a hash by our authentication provider. Why: to provide your account and sign the app in. Legal basis: contract (our Terms).
  • Sign-in sessions: session ID, when it was created and last used, and the device or browser type, at sign-in and when the app checks your session. Why: so you can see and end your sessions, and for account security. Legal basis: contract; legitimate interest (security).
  • Security events: sign-in attempts and their outcome, and the IP address, at sign-in and security changes. Why: to protect your account and the service from abuse. Legal basis: legitimate interest.
  • Feedback you send: a category (bug, idea, general) and your text, up to 4,000 characters, linked to your account, only when you tick the consent box and press Send. Before sending, the app removes keys, tokens, email addresses and personal folder paths from the text. If you add diagnostics, you see them in the text before you send. Why: to fix problems and improve the product. Legal basis: consent.
  • Usage signals: "app opened", "sign-in started", "sign-in succeeded" and "first successful run", with the time, app version, operating system and a random installation ID, only if you turn on usage statistics (off by default). Why: to understand whether the app works for people. Legal basis: consent.
  • Your account choices (for example your statistics consent) and any deletion request, when you make them. Why: to honour them. Legal basis: legal obligation; contract.

We never receive your prompts, conversations, files, code, API keys or screenshots.

4. What the app downloads without sending us anything about you

  • Update checks. Every 30 minutes the app downloads a public, signed update file from our releases page on GitHub. It sends no identifier of yours; GitHub sees your IP address, as with any download. The same signed file lets us switch off a risky feature (for example Computer Use) remotely if it misbehaves, without a new version.
  • Downloads you ask for. AI tools (from npm), local models (from Hugging Face), local engines (from GitHub), voice models (from Hugging Face and GitHub, after you confirm), and Microsoft WebView2 at install time if your PC lacks it. These servers see your IP address, as with any download.

5. Data that goes directly to other companies, when you choose

When you use these features, data goes directly from your computer to that company, under its own terms. We do not receive it.

  • The AI provider you pick (for example Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, DeepSeek, xAI): your messages, the files the agent reads, and tool results.
  • Model lists: the app asks your provider for its list of models, using your key.
  • Claude usage limits: if you use Claude Code, the app reads the sign-in credentials Claude Code saved on your computer, only to ask Anthropic how much of your own plan you have used. We do not store, log or send those credentials anywhere else.
  • Computer Use (beta, off until you turn it on and approve it): screenshots and the window layout of the app being controlled go to the model you picked.
  • Voice dictation: runs on your computer by default. Only if you choose so, the audio clip goes to Groq or OpenAI with your key.
  • Connectors and GitHub (for example Google, GitHub, Vercel, Figma): only what the features you use need.
  • The in-app browser: the websites you visit.
  • Images, charts and videos inside answers. An answer can contain images or charts hosted on other websites, chosen by the model, not by us. Images load automatically when the answer appears (without cookies and without telling the site where you came from), so that site sees your IP address. Interactive charts may load libraries from jsDelivr, cdnjs or unpkg and fonts from Google Fonts. Videos (YouTube, Vimeo and similar) load only after you press play; the platform then sees your IP address and may use its own cookies.

6. Data that stays on your computer

Your conversations and run history, preferences, Computer Use rules, voice transcripts and the app log (rotated, about 100 MB at most, with passwords and tokens masked). API keys, connector sign-ins and your account session are stored encrypted, tied to your Windows account. Your account session works offline for at most 30 days, then the app asks you to sign in again. You can delete all of this from the app or by uninstalling it and removing its folder. The AI tools themselves (Claude Code, Codex and others) keep their own data in their own folders.

8. Website and cookies

The website is served by GitHub Pages through Cloudflare; they see visitors’ IP addresses. The rest of this section describes the website’s storage and its optional, first-party statistics.

Essential storage and your choice

The first-party codevalanche_consent cookie remembers your explicit website analytics choice for 180 days. Loading a page does not renew it. The codevalanche_route_checked cookie prevents repeated country routing for one year, and codevalanche_language remembers the language selected in the footer for account pages. These essential preferences do not enable analytics. Theme preferences remain in codevalanche_theme. Essential account and security storage works with any analytics choice.

Optional website analytics

No Google Analytics is loaded. Our first-party SDK sends observations only after your acceptance and a valid consent receipt. The recipient is Radovar, operating Codevalanche, with qualified Supabase infrastructure as a processor. No external analytics dashboard or third-party tracker is required.

Optional observations include registered pages, section reach, estimated visible and active time, scroll buckets, feature and FAQ choices, account entry intent, download clicks and supported browser performance measurements. They do not prove reading, completed downloads or installation. Account activity has a separate signed-in choice, verified against the current account and consent state. Website acceptance cannot enable it.

Only allowlisted campaign parameters (source, medium, campaign, content and term) and the normalized external referrer domain may be used after consent. Unsafe values are rejected. Missing or same-origin referrers remain unknown. We send coarse device, operating-system and browser families, viewport buckets and locale, never the raw user agent, exact screen size or a country inferred by an external lookup. Raw URLs, queries, fragments, advertising click IDs, form values, email addresses, prompts, source code, project paths and credentials are excluded.

Identifiers, retention and withdrawal

After consent and receipt validation, an anonymous browser ID lasts up to 30 days; a session lasts up to 24 hours with a 30-minute idle limit. Website IDs and queues use first-party owned_analytics:codevalanche storage. Queues are bounded to 100 observations younger than 24 hours. Delivery is best effort and can fail on navigation, process loss or unavailable storage. Raw analytics retention is 90 days; non-identifying aggregates are retained for 13 months when the qualified service is activated.

Manage cookies is always available on these pages. Declining or withdrawing immediately stops optional emission and clears optional IDs and queued observations. An offline revocation retains only the receipt revocation proof until acknowledgement. Withdrawal does not retroactively erase accepted observations; deletion requests follow the separate privacy process. Website history is never merged into a later account.

Account activity and security

Account activity analytics requires a separate explicit choice in your signed-in account. Website analytics acceptance does not enable account attribution. Events include an opaque event and installation ID, verified account ID, platform, app version and timestamps. Activity such as app openings or remote reconnects is separated from confirmed provider or execution receipts. Client reports alone do not prove successful runs. No prompts, source code, file paths, passwords, tokens or recovery codes are included.

Withdrawing account analytics consent stops new collection and rejects queued events from an earlier consent choice. Guest activity is not automatically attached to an account, and anonymous history is not merged into account activity. Optional website-account observations use a separate receipt and can include profile-page views, profile-edit and save intentions, logout clicks and estimated active time; profile values and credentials are excluded. Intentions are separate from confirmed operational outcomes.

Supabase authentication and request logs have separate provider retention settings. We do not promise a fixed retention period for these logs. Deleting application records does not promise deletion of provider copies or local projects.

Profile information is functional account data, separate from optional analytics. Detailed profile-save reporting is not available in this release. Contact us about account-data deletion.

Download clicks, gateway redirects and provider counter changes are different observations, not completed transfers, installations or activations. Unqualified release, delivery and native sources remain unavailable for measurement. GitHub Pages hosts this website. Cloudflare is configured for DNS and, when enabled for website traffic, delivery and security. These services may process IP addresses and request details separately from optional measurement, even if you decline it. Provider logs have separate retention settings. Cloudflare RUM and Google Analytics are disabled.

The current account pages do not offer security-history or administrative audit reports. Limited operational security records may still be kept to protect account access, separately from optional analytics.

Download page counts

On the download pages we count, without cookies and without any identifier, how many times each page is opened and how many downloads start. For each day we keep only a number per page, per platform as your browser reports it (for example Windows or macOS) and per site language. Nothing is stored on your device, and we do not keep your IP address, browser details or anything else that could identify you. These counts work the same whatever you choose for optional analytics.

The count is sent to our database at Supabase, which sees your IP address while it handles the request, as with any server. If your browser sends Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track, you are not counted. The installer itself is downloaded from GitHub, which sees your IP address, as with any download. Legal basis: legitimate interest (knowing whether downloads work and which platforms to build next).

Your choice

You can decline analytics, or change your preference at any time using Manage cookies in the account page or homepage footer. Blocking or deleting cookies in your browser also removes the stored preference.

9. Companies that process data for us

  • Supabase: accounts, sign-in and database. Region: EU (Frankfurt).
  • GoDaddy: sending account emails (confirmation, password reset).
  • Cloudflare: website delivery and security.
  • GitHub (Microsoft): hosting the website and the update files.
  • Google: Sign in with Google, if you choose it.

Some of these companies are based outside the European Economic Area, for example in the United States. Where that applies, transfers rely on the EU–US Data Privacy Framework for certified companies or on the European Commission’s standard contractual clauses.

We disclose data to authorities only when the law requires it.

10. How long we keep data

  • Account and profile: as long as you keep the account.
  • Usage signals and feedback: until you delete your account.
  • Security events: IP address 7 days, the rest 90 days.
  • Website statistics: as described in section 8.

11. Deleting your account

You can ask to delete your account from the account page. All your sessions end at once, and we delete the account and the data linked to it within 30 days. Security events remain for the periods above to protect the service. Data on your computer is yours to delete, from the app or by uninstalling it.

12. Your rights

You can ask us to access, copy, correct or delete your data, to restrict its use, or object to use based on legitimate interest, and you can withdraw consent at any time (this does not affect what happened before). From the account page you can edit your profile, see and end sessions, turn statistics off and delete your account. For anything else, write to info@radovar.com. We answer within one month.

You can also complain to your data protection authority. In Romania that is ANSPDCP (www.dataprotection.ro).

13. Security

Encrypted connections, database access only through our server with session checks, keys and tokens encrypted on your computer, and signed updates that the app checks before installing. No system is perfect: if you find a security issue, please write to security@radovar.com.

14. Children

Codevalanche is not meant for anyone under 16, and we do not knowingly collect their data.

15. Changes

When we change this policy we update the date below. We announce important changes in the app or by email before they apply.
